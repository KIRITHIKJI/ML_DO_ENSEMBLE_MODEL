# Pre-registration: testing the deployed models on 2026

**Written 2026-10-18, before any 2026 dissolved-oxygen value was read.**

This file is committed on its own, before the 2026 data exists on disk and
before `results/live_2026/` exists at all, so that the order in `git log`
shows the plan came first. `tests/test_deploy.py` checks that order.

The 2025 test (`reports/final_results.md`) was a test because `FROZEN.md`
was committed before it. This is the same arrangement, one year on: the
system is already fixed, the models are already trained, and the only thing
left to decide is what counts as a pass. That is decided here.

---

## 1. What is being tested

`models/final/` - the five members (random forest, XGBoost, LightGBM,
BiLSTM, CNN-LSTM) retrained on **2023 + 2024 + 2025** with the frozen
hyperparameters and the frozen round and epoch counts from `FROZEN.md`,
combined by the step-7 **stacking** meta-model, with the **frozen warning
thresholds** from `models/ensemble/warning_thresholds.json`.

These models have never been scored. They were built in step 8.4 so the app
could forecast using every hour ever recorded, and the final report says
plainly that they are "untested and untestable" because 2025 - the only
hold-out that existed - was inside their training data. 2026 is the year
that makes them testable.

**Nothing will be retuned on 2026.** No hyperparameter, no threshold, no
ensemble choice, no model selection. The models are used exactly as they
sit on disk. `models/final` is identified in every output by a version id
that is the SHA-256 over the per-file hashes of that folder, so a later
reader can check that the models scored here are the models that were
deployed.

## 2. The test period

**2026-01-01 00:00 UTC to the last complete day for which ERA5 is
available.**

Open-Meteo's ERA5 archive runs about five days behind the present, so
"available" is resolved as `today (UTC) - 5 days`, and then trimmed further
to the last day on which every ERA5 variable is complete for all 24 hours
at every station. Resolved on 2026-10-18, that is:

> **2026-01-01 .. 2026-10-13** (286 days)

`config_deploy.yaml` records this as `test_2026.end_frozen`. The scorer
will refuse a window that ends later than this date, so the period cannot
be quietly extended to a month that happens to score better.

Hours after 2026-10-13 are not part of this test. They are used only for
the live-data penalty (section 6) and for ordinary production runs.

## 3. The data

The **same three sources, through the same code paths, as training**:

| source | what | how |
|---|---|---|
| USGS instantaneous values | DO, water temperature, conductivity, pH, turbidity, discharge | `src/data/download.fetch_water` |
| ERA5 via Open-Meteo archive | 8 weather variables | `src/data/download.fetch_open_meteo("weather", ...)` |
| CAMS via Open-Meteo | 8 air-quality variables | `src/data/download.fetch_open_meteo("air", ...)` |

Cleaning is `src/data/clean.clean_station` and the features are
`src/features/build_features.build_station` - the project's own functions,
not new copies of them. The 211 features, the 72-hour sequences, the class
boundaries (Low < 5.0, Medium to 7.0, Good above) and the horizons
(6, 12, 24, 48 h) are whatever `config.yaml` says, unchanged.

Two deliberate differences from a naive re-run, both decided here:

1. **Two weeks of history before 2026-01-01 are fetched** (`preroll_hours:
   336`) so that every lag and rolling window - the longest is a 168-hour
   rain sum - is full on the first scored hour. Those hours are inputs
   only; no row before 2026-01-01 is scored.
2. **The per-station variable drops decided in step 2 are reused**
   (`data/interim/dropped_variables.csv`) rather than re-decided on 2026. A
   model must be fed the same columns it was trained on; re-deciding could
   hand it a column that was all-NaN during training.

## 4. The same 30 stations

The 30 stations in `data/raw/stations.csv`. No station is added and none is
swapped for an easier one.

A station **will** be dropped from the 2026 scoring if its DO sensor did
not report in 2026, or if an API refuses its data - that is an operational
fact, not a choice, and every such station will be listed with its reason
in `results/live_2026/skipped.csv` and in the report. One station
(`05474500`) already stopped reporting on 2025-11-19, so a reduced station
count is expected. **The headline 2026 numbers will be reported over
whatever stations actually reported, with the count stated**, and the
per-station table will show every one of them.

## 5. Metrics - the same ones, nothing new

Scored with `src/evaluation/metrics.py` as it stands:

- **Regression**: R2, RMSE, MAE, NSE, skill vs persistence, danger-zone
  RMSE (true DO in 3-7 mg/L).
- **Classification** (Low / Medium / Good): accuracy, balanced accuracy,
  macro F1, ROC-AUC, per-class recall - under **both** the frozen warning
  rule (P(Low) >= tau) and argmax.
- **Early warning**: onset recall, onset precision, false-alarm rate,
  transition accuracy.
- **Lead time**: events defined exactly as in 2025 (DO drops below
  5.0 mg/L after at least 24 h at or above it; a warning counts if the
  24-hour rule fired in the 24 h before the onset).
- **Uncertainty**: 1,000 **station-week** bootstrap resamples, the
  `ensemble.bootstrap` setting from `config.yaml`.
- **Per station** and **all four horizons**, plus persistence and each
  member for context.

## 6. The live-data penalty

A deployed system cannot wait five days for ERA5. On the last **30 days**
of the test window, the **same hours** will be scored twice:

- once with ERA5 archive weather and CAMS, as the 2026 test proper does;
- once with the Open-Meteo forecast API's recent past (model
  `ecmwf_ifs025`, the one that carries `soil_moisture_0_to_7cm`) and the
  CAMS forecast feed.

Water data is identical in both. The difference in RMSE, macro F1, onset
recall and false-alarm rate is the price of running live, and it will be
reported whatever its sign. Every hour in every live file is tagged with
the source it came from, so no forecast can later be mistaken for a
reanalysis one.

## 7. Success criteria - fixed now

### 7.1 The hard promise

> **The false-alarm rate at 24 hours must be at or below 2%.**

This is the budget the system has promised since step 3
(`warning_rule.max_false_alarm_rate` = 0.02) and the number the 2025 test
was judged against. It is a pass/fail criterion, not a comparison. A
false-alarm rate above 2% at 24 h is a **failure of the deployed system**
and will be reported as one.

Reported alongside, but not pass/fail: the false-alarm rate at 6, 12 and
48 h.

### 7.2 Onset recall and RMSE against the 2025 intervals

The deployed models are judged against the 2025 result **as an interval,
not as a point**. The reference is the 95% station-week bootstrap interval
of the frozen 2025 stacking system (computed on 2025 only, from
`results/test/pred_stacking_h*.parquet`, before any 2026 value was read):

| horizon | RMSE 2025 | RMSE 95% CI | onset recall 2025 | onset recall 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 h | 0.5146 | [0.4908, 0.5378] | 0.8608 | [0.8424, 0.8782] |
| 12 h | 0.5885 | [0.5602, 0.6156] | 0.8603 | [0.8395, 0.8791] |
| 24 h | 0.6719 | [0.6407, 0.7011] | 0.4960 | [0.4626, 0.5310] |
| 48 h | 0.8534 | [0.8188, 0.8848] | 0.4852 | [0.4473, 0.5235] |

*(macro F1 and false alarms, for reference: macro F1 0.9050
[0.8998, 0.9103] at 6 h, 0.8970 [0.8911, 0.9034] at 12 h, 0.8858
[0.8794, 0.8922] at 24 h, 0.8561 [0.8480, 0.8645] at 48 h; false alarms
0.0175 [0.0155, 0.0198], 0.0173 [0.0153, 0.0194], 0.0151 [0.0132,
0.0171], 0.0133 [0.0113, 0.0153].)*

The verdict for each horizon and each of the two metrics is one of three
words, decided by where the 2026 point estimate falls:

- **holds** - inside the 2025 interval (RMSE no higher than the upper
  bound; onset recall no lower than the lower bound), or better than it.
- **degraded** - outside the interval in the worse direction (RMSE above
  the upper bound, or onset recall below the lower bound).
- **improved** - outside the interval in the better direction (RMSE below
  the lower bound, or onset recall above the upper bound).

### 7.3 The overall verdict, defined now

- **PASS** - the 24 h false alarm rate is at or below 2%, **and** at 24 h
  neither RMSE nor onset recall is *degraded*.
- **PARTIAL** - the false-alarm promise holds, but RMSE or onset recall at
  24 h is degraded.
- **FAIL** - the 24 h false alarm rate is above 2%.

24 hours is the horizon the project has reported as its headline since
step 3, which is why the overall verdict rests on it. The other three
horizons are reported in full and can turn a PASS into a noted weakness,
but they do not change the word.

### 7.4 What a degraded result would mean

It would not be a licence to retune. The frozen system is what it is, and
a worse 2026 is a measurement of how it transfers to a year nobody
prepared for - which is the only honest thing a deployed system can be
asked. Retraining on 2026 is a separate, gated operation
(`src/deploy/retrain.py`), and its promotion gate compares candidates on
months they did not train on, never on this test.

## 8. Things that would invalidate this test

Stated now, so they cannot be invoked selectively afterwards:

- **Fewer than 20 of the 30 stations report usable 2026 DO.** Then the
  station set is too different from 2025's for the comparison to mean much;
  the numbers will still be reported, with the comparison marked
  unreliable.
- **A model file in `models/final` changes.** The version id in every
  output would differ from the one in `models/final/MODEL_CARD.md`, and the
  test would have to be re-run and re-labelled.
- **The ERA5 window is extended past 2026-10-13.** The scorer refuses it.
